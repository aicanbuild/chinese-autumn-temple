# Visibility of refined roof and caisson geometry

The full Blender geometry remains unchanged. Four large material meshes (the ceramic roof and three caisson materials) now carry 90 logical spatial cells. Whole triangles are assigned by their centroid; each cell's actual bounds enclose every vertex, including triangles crossing cell boundaries. Main-camera frustum tests select a subsequence of the original indices, retaining original triangle order, vertex buffers, UVs, normals, materials and one draw per material. No surface is simplified.

The filtered index is reused until the cell visibility mask changes. It is installed only during the main-camera draw and restored afterward. Other cameras and shadow passes receive the original complete index. Geometry/position/index changes and unsupported draw ranges fall back to the original mesh. This adds one capacity-sized index buffer per eligible mesh plus CPU cell/range records, and small frustum tests when the view moves. It does not reduce asset download size. The buffers share the page/scene lifetime. The previous candidate that made many separate meshes was discarded because it increased draw calls.

The Blender-derived hall occluders were also regenerated from the current native interior. Their previous beam rectangles predated the thinner caisson wood. The extraction now records SHA-256 bindings for both the base sanctuary and replacement caisson; the portable test detects stale geometry bindings after later edits. Native source files remain unmodified by the extractor.

`static-mesh-cells-fixture.html` compares original and selected-index rendering of the actual temple, sanctuary, roof, caisson and woodland with fresh sun and sanctuary spotlight shadows. All six final images are byte-identical and all draw-call counts are unchanged. Submitted triangles decrease by 205,610 at the front interior view, 125,292 at the ceiling view, 1,003,865 at the side view and 394,977 looking out of the doorway. The two broad exterior views retain their complete counts. This fixture omits water captures and postprocessing; it does not prove a whole-app FPS improvement.
