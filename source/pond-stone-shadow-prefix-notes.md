# Excluding pond stones from unrelated lamp shadows

The sanctuary spotlight does not reach any of the 257 pond stones, but its frustum overlaps the large combined bounds of seven stone batches. Those batches previously submitted 178 complete instances (518,240 triangles) to that shadow map. The sunlight still needs all 257 stones.

The existing conservative instanced-shadow helper now has an order-preserving mode, selected only for the pond stones. It computes the last potentially visible instance for each light and temporarily limits the shadow draw to that prefix. It can retain extra instances, never discard one whose padded bounds intersect the light's frustum. A zero prefix avoids the entire draw. Positions, geometry, colors and instance-buffer order remain unchanged, so the pond's separate reflection prefix remains valid. This adds no mesh, map, material, buffer upload or shader variant.

Full counts are restored after the shadow draw. Geometry/attribute edits, instance transforms, colors, count changes, world transforms, padding and light-view changes invalidate cached prefixes; displacement materials and unknown shadow cameras fall back to complete draws. Existing vegetation keeps its original sorting mode. The new unit test checks byte-preserved matrix/color arrays, independent light prefixes, unknown/temporary counts and restoration.

Six actual temple cameras at two wind times produced twelve byte-identical final color images, sun shadow maps and sanctuary spotlight maps. Each fixture draw that refreshes the spotlight removes 518,240 submitted triangles and seven calls. The fixture deliberately refreshes both lights each frame; the app refreshes the spotlight at about 2 Hz, so this saving is **per spotlight update**, not every color frame. All 224 automated tests pass. Evidence: `evidence/pond-stone-shadow-prefix-browser.json`.

An alternating timing rerun with no tests running collected 36 completed GPU samples per mode, with zero discarded queries. Full/optimized GPU means were 36.637/30.035 ms, medians 8.080/9.646 ms; CPU medians were both 1.2 ms (33 samples after three warm-up exclusions). The large spread and conflicting median/mean directions do **not** establish a stable speed or FPS gain. Only the redundant-submission reduction is demonstrated. See `evidence/pond-stone-shadow-prefix-timing.json`.

Integrated preview verification on runtime `98eff92e1800` confirmed eight batches, 257 stones and light prefixes `[0,257]`. Pickup, sweeping (27 removed leaves), return, hall entry and incense burning worked; warning/error logs were empty. `evidence/pond-stone-shadow-prefix-main.json` and `.png` retain this check. A sampled frame can report zero skipped stone triangles because the spotlight is not updated every frame.

## Measured distance-mesh experiment, not deployed

`build_pond_stone_lod.py` produces six standalone Blender distance prototypes in `source/池岸远景.blend`, with source normals and moss colors transferred onto the reduced surface. Original 5,120-triangle prototypes reduce to 2,304–3,840 triangles, subject to bidirectional vertex/centroid/edge-midpoint distance samples below 4 mm in prototype space. An 8 mm runtime estimate with a 0.4/0.5 pixel hysteresis was tested. These samples are not a rigorous continuous Hausdorff bound.

Reopening the native file confirmed six closed, positive-volume meshes, custom normals and point colors; hashes confirm the original native stone file and original GLB are unchanged. The portable experiment model is 167,128 bytes with no new textures, stored in `source/stone-detail-assets/`, outside the published static directory. Browser candidates are the four `*-stone-experiment.js` modules and the `pond-stone-detail-*` fixtures, also outside the production module graph.

The experimental BatchedMesh path reduced 725,344–1,402,720 triangle submissions across the sampled views. After correcting the batch transform in caustic coordinates, close views were byte-identical or differed by one channel value, and far views had small image regions changed by LOD. Spotlight maps matched; sun depth differences stayed below 2.802e-6. However, an alternating timing run did not demonstrate reliable speed improvement: GPU means 306.924/308.510 ms and medians 168.626/235.754 ms, with large outliers and additional system load. Therefore neither the reduced meshes nor that batching path is used in the published app. The original high-detail instanced geometry and water shaders remain in production.

Native/build/visual/performance evidence is retained under `evidence/pond-stone-detail-*`. The accepted order-preserving shadow optimization is independent of this rejected rendering experiment. Whole-scene commercial AAA fidelity and frame rate remain incomplete.
