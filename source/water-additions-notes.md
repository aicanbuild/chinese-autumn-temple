# East creek and entrance handwashing basin

The reference was the user's local screen recording, sampled at eight-second intervals into `evidence/stream-reference`. The creek reproduces observed shallow clarity, separate currents, stone wakes and moving light; no original code was available.

## Creek

`build_temple_stream.py` authors a 38 m curved creek east of the existing pond, cuts only its terrain corridor, and creates the gravel bed, 22 emerged stones, 154 bank stones, 2,300 pebbles and fine sedges. The water shares the pond's reflection/refraction captures at Y = -0.025. A separate vector atlas bends motion around the authored rocks; two phased normal samples crossfade without reset jumps. Creek extinction is lower than the pond and directional caustics retain the sunlight shadow mask. Disjoint surface bounds keep the two water areas from unnecessarily keeping captures active between them.

`fix_stream_terrain_export.py` retains the existing terrain's authored 16-bit vertex color semantic when Blender emits an extra white COLOR_0. Its original roughness is retained. The remaining terrain geometry is unchanged outside the corridor.

## Handwashing basin

`build_handwash.py` authors a hollow granite bowl, carved lotus frieze, bamboo inlet and rest, drain pebbles, and a hollow hinoki ladle. Native source parts remain separately editable; browser geometry is batched by material and assembly. Original procedural grain assets are generated by `paint_handwash_materials.py`.

The basin is at (-3.7, 0.20, 15.6), moved forward to clear the existing pine needles without changing that tree. At the bridge entrance, click the basin/ladle to focus and pick up. Mouse movement or a touch drag stirs the water. Click to dip and lift a filled ladle, click again to pour, then stir again. The toolbar's return action or Escape restores the ladle and prior camera. Picking a different tool also returns it. Ripple impulses follow the ladle contact position; a separate periodic impulse belongs to the bamboo water jet. No floating world labels were added.

The user's correction is encoded in `ladleTilt`: the handle extends toward +Z, so negative X rotation raises the thin end and lowers the opposite lip. The pouring jet originates from that lowered lip. A regression test checks that direction geometrically.

The basin uses a translucent, reflective physical water surface with vertex ripple displacement. It does not run a full fluid simulation or an additional full-scene refraction capture. There are no modeled hands. The original temple-wide AAA visual goal remains unfinished.
