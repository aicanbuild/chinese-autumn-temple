# Sanctuary floor contact visibility

The existing room probe and spot/sun shadows left furniture feet and shaded wall bases too uniformly lit. The floor now has a separate, static AO atlas derived from the furnished Blender temple. It strengthens the transition below the altar, side tables, turned candle stands and incense vessel without baking their color or replacing direct light.

`bake_hall_contact.py` reads `听秋寺院.blend` through library loading, evaluates the mesh geometry and retains surfaces conservatively intersecting the floor's one-metre upward AO neighborhood. The copied occluder contains 409853 polygons from 999 mesh objects. A separate horizontal receiver at native Z = 1.0702 m sits 0.2 mm above the authored stone top. This intentionally measures furnishing/wall contact; it does not resolve individual 2.5 mm slab bevels or the 8 mm joint recess. The original temple and paving documents are not rewritten.

Cycles emission-bakes the Ambient Occlusion node at 16 samples with 64 AO samples and a 1 m distance. The resulting non-color PNG is 1536 × 1024, 769195 bytes (751 KiB); the local editable `source/殿内地面接触光照.blend` includes the occluder and packed atlas. Baking took 265 seconds on this run. It is an ambient visibility approximation, not spatial global illumination.

`verify_hall_contact.py` reopens that document and checks 145 floor/altar-edge locations using 2048 independent cosine-weighted BVH rays each. One point falls inside a pillar right beside a texel boundary: a point-ray reference differs by 0.1549 because the rendered texture averages adjacent areas. That outlier is additionally checked against an 8×8 area sample per neighboring texel, 512 rays each, with the actual bilinear weights. The final mean absolute AO-factor difference is 0.0022675, maximum 0.030726. Both the raw point comparison and filtered comparison remain in the evidence; the test tolerance was not relaxed. Asset hashes in `hall-contact-ao.json` require rebaking after relevant geometry changes.

The runtime assigns an independent world-aligned `uv1` to all three floor materials and applies the atlas at 0.8 intensity. Original rotated slab UVs, color/normal/roughness maps and geometry remain intact. Three's standard AO path attenuates indirect lighting; the existing room-probe capture includes the finished floor. This adds a mipmapped atlas and one texture lookup on floor fragments, no model triangles, draw batches, lights or repeated captures.

The real-WebGL fixture compares four actual interior camera views with fixed lights and room probe. Only darkening occurs, localized according to the atlas; spotlight shadow bytes, triangles and draw counts are identical. With floor environment/hemisphere lighting removed, final color bytes are identical too, confirming direct-light isolation. The fixture uses 2048 sun shadows and omits full-scene postprocessing; the integrated app is also checked visually. Evidence is in `evidence/hall-contact-browser.json`, ray-audit/bake reports and before/after screenshots. The full suite passes 208 checks.

This is tied to the current static furnishings. Moving furniture or changing the walls requires rebaking. It does not supply contact AO on the furniture itself, dynamic object contact, diffuse color bleeding or ray-traced reflections. Whole-scene commercial AAA fidelity and performance remain unproven and incomplete.
